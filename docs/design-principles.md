# Design Principles

## Purpose

The TWNY Proposal Platform should feel premium, calm, and credible. Each client route is a crafted environment that supports trust, clarity, and momentum. The design goal is not spectacle for its own sake. It is refined presentation in service of a proposal and, where relevant, a live demo.

## What The Platform Should Do

### Use Restrained Premium Design

The visual language should feel considered and expensive without becoming glossy or theatrical. Interfaces should communicate confidence through proportion, pacing, materials, and copy discipline.

### Use Editorial Typography

Typography should carry meaning. Headings, body copy, supporting notes, and strategic callouts should feel composed rather than default. The aim is an editorial reading experience, not a dashboard.

### Use Architectural Spacing

Spacing should create structure, rhythm, and calm. Layouts should breathe. Sections should feel intentional, with clear visual hierarchy and enough room for content to land properly.

### Use Grounded Shadows And Elevation

Shadows and layers should help establish depth and material presence. They should feel grounded and restrained, not glowing or decorative. Elevation should support credibility.

### Use Calm Motion

Motion should clarify transitions, guide attention, and add polish. Animations should be smooth and measured. The experience should feel composed during navigation and interaction, not restless.

### Keep Copy Believable

Proposal language should sound human, informed, and specific. The platform should support clear recommendations, sensible claims, and real business framing rather than inflated marketing language.

### Stay Premium But Approachable

The work should feel elevated without becoming cold. Warmth matters. The best routes should combine clarity, confidence, and hospitality.

### Prioritise Experiential Refinement

The quality bar should come from the total experience: pacing, transitions, copy, spacing, hierarchy, and demo integration. Small refinements matter more than flashy UI tricks.

## What The Platform Should Avoid

### Avoid SaaS Aesthetics

The platform should not look like a startup dashboard, product marketing site, admin panel, or generic component-library demo. Avoid visual patterns that signal software productisation over curated proposal delivery.

### Avoid Over-Animation

Do not use motion as decoration. Excessive animation slows comprehension and cheapens the experience. Movement should never compete with the content.

### Avoid Generic AI-Generated Copy

Avoid language that feels synthetic, padded, or interchangeable. Empty strategic phrasing, abstract claims, and generic transformation language reduce trust quickly.

### Avoid Excessive Glow And Effects

Heavy glows, oversized blurs, aggressive shadows, and hyper-polished visual effects make the interface feel trend-led rather than grounded. Depth should be subtle and believable.

### Avoid Overly Corporate Language

Do not default to enterprise jargon, consulting clichés, or sterile sales phrasing. The voice should be clear, intelligent, and warm.

### Avoid Trendy UI Gimmicks

Parallax for its own sake, novelty cursors, overbuilt transitions, and performative interactivity should be treated skeptically. If an idea does not improve comprehension, persuasion, or delight in a meaningful way, it likely should not ship.

## Experience Standard

Every route should aim to feel:

- calm
- precise
- premium
- human
- client-specific
- strategically credible

The experience should help a client understand the thinking, feel the quality of execution, and move naturally between proposal content and any supporting demo material.

## Practical Design Test

Before shipping a route, the team should be able to say:

- it feels like a crafted TWNY proposal, not a generic web template
- the copy sounds believable and specific
- the interaction design is polished but restrained
- the spacing and typography carry the experience
- the route supports trust instead of trying to impress through noise
