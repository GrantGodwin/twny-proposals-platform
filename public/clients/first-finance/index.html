A practical website update from twny

We built the original First Finance site years ago. It worked well for the business then. The business has grown since.

At the time, the site did what it needed to do: give referred clients a clear place to understand the basics and feel they were in the right hands.

Since then, First Finance has changed. Craig and Ben now carry a deeper story: family continuity, long client relationships, industry recognition, and a practice with more depth than the website currently shows. This is about bringing the site up to where the business already is.

What has changed

The current site is showing an older version of First Finance.

That happens naturally. Good businesses keep moving; websites usually do not unless we come back to them.

  1. 01

    The original brief was simpler.

    The existing site was right for a straightforward online presence. It now needs to show more of what First Finance has become: thirty-three years in practice, a mixed residential and commercial book, and clients who have stayed for decades.

  2. 02

    Ben is now part of the front door.

    This is no longer just a succession detail in the background. For clients, it says the relationship has continuity beyond one person.

  3. 03

    Referred clients still check the site.

    The referral still does the heavy lifting. The website just needs to back it up: enough clarity, proof, and warmth for people to feel comfortable taking the next step.

  4. 04

    The proof is already there.

    Craig's Elite Broker recognition, Ben's awards, the lender panel, and the long-term testimonials are all real. They do not need to be pushed hard. They just need to be easier to see.

  5. 05

    Different clients need different entry points.

    A refinancing family, a portfolio investor, and a business owner buying premises are not looking for the same first answer. The site can make those paths clearer without making the business feel bigger or colder.

Why it matters

The issue is fit, not effort.

The current website reflects the business we built it for at the time. It now needs to show First Finance as people experience it today: established, family-led, commercially capable, and still personal.

That matters because the website sits alongside referrals, phone calls, accountant introductions, and long-standing client trust. It should not replace any of that. It should reduce doubt and make the first conversation easier.

What should carry forward

The update should protect what clients already trust.

We do not need to make First Finance feel like a different business. We need to make the real business easier to understand.

Already there · 01

Thirty-three years of practice.

Craig established First Finance in 1992 after eleven years at ANZ. That history should be easy to see without turning it into a speech.

Already there · 02

A genuine family-led handover.

Ben joined in 2015 and is now a clear part of the client relationship. That gives the business a future as well as a history.

Already there · 03

A real award shelf.

Craig's Elite Broker recognition and Ben's awards deserve clearer placement. Presented plainly, they add confidence without sounding like bragging.

Already there · 04

A purple-and-gold identity already in place.

The existing purple and gold already belong to the business. The update should refine that equity, not walk away from it.

What we would update

A clearer site for the business First Finance already is.

  1. 01

    Bring Craig and Ben forward.

    Craig and Ben should be visible early because they explain the continuity clients are really buying into.

  2. 02

    Show the credentials simply.

    Recognition, licences, lender breadth, and experience should be easy to find. They should feel useful, not decorative.

  3. 03

    Treat testimonials as relationships.

    The existing client words are strong. A little context around timeframes and situations would make them feel more like real relationships and less like generic testimonials.

  4. 04

    Organise services around real situations.

    Home finance, portfolio finance, and business finance each need a different first answer. The site should help people find the right starting point quickly.

  5. 05

    Add useful working surfaces.

    A borrowing estimate, refinance health check, process guide, and occasional market note would make the site more useful before someone picks up the phone.

How the identity could be refreshed

Keep the recognisable cues. Make them feel current and useful.

The useful move is not to throw away what people recognise. It is to clean it up and use it more consistently.

About

Two brokers. One client book. Thirty-three years.

First Finance was established in 1992. Ben joined in 2015. The practice is built to outlast either of us — by design.

In 1990, in the middle of the "recession we had to have," Craig Walker left ANZ after eleven years in business and commercial banking. He'd planned to move into private enterprise. Within two years he'd started First Finance, joining a small handful of Melbourne brokers operating before the industry had front and trail commissions, before residential broking was structured at all, before most of the lenders First Finance now works with existed.

For most of the '90s and early 2000s the focus shifted toward residential — the trail book was the security. The commercial work stayed, then grew. By the 2010s, First Finance had become the kind of practice that didn't need to market: clients came back, and then sent their kids. Ben joined in 2015 with a Bachelor of Business and a Diploma of Finance and Mortgage Broking. He spent the decade since building his own award shelf — Buyers Choice Asset Finance Broker of the Year 2021, an Australian Mortgage Excellence Award for Productivity, an MFAA Young Professional state finalist nomination in 2022, the Buyers Choice MD Award.

In August 2024 Craig was profiled by The Adviser as an Elite Broker — a six-minute feature and a podcast appearance on what it takes to run a full-service brokerage. He's now in his thirty-sixth year in finance, his twenty-fifth as a broker, and gradually transitioning the day-to-day client work to Ben while staying full-time in commercial.

Process

What working with us actually looks like.

Five steps. Honest about timing. Honest about who does what.

01

An initial conversation.

Twenty to thirty minutes. We listen, you talk. We confirm whether we're the right fit before either of us spends more time. No paperwork, no commitment, no rush — most of our long-term clients start here.

02

A picture of your position.

We gather your numbers — income, expenses, existing debt, assets — and give you a realistic picture of your borrowing position. If something needs to change before lenders will take it seriously, we tell you now, not later.

03

A short list of lenders.

We narrow 35+ lender options down to the two or three that genuinely suit your situation. We tell you why. We're not optimising for the highest commission — we're optimising for the loan you'll still be happy with in three years.

04

Application and approval.

We prepare and submit. We chase the bank, not you. If a condition comes back that we should have spotted in step two, that's on us — and we deal with it.

05

Settlement and after.

We coordinate settlement. We stay your broker after — including the conversation about whether to refinance two or three years from now. Most of our clients first met us a decade or more ago. That's the work.

Insights

A quarterly note from us.

Not a marketing blog. A short piece of writing every three months on what lender appetite or policy is actually doing.

Q2 · 2026

What lender appetite has actually looked like through the first half of 2026.

— Ben Walker

Through the first six months of 2026, lender appetite for owner-occupier refinance has tightened from the second-tier banks while the majors have quietly become more competitive on cashback. Investment lending policy remains stricter than most borrowers expect, particularly around rental yield assumptions.

The shift to watch is the major banks reintroducing structured cashbacks for refinance — $2,000 to $4,000 range — without the policy tightening that accompanied the last round in 2023. For clients with clean serviceability the math is again worth running.

On the investment side, three lenders have pulled back from high-LVR investment lending in the past quarter. Two have come back in cautiously. Anyone planning a third or fourth property purchase in the next six months should run serviceability before assuming what was possible twelve months ago is still possible now.

Worth a short conversation if you're either refinancing in the next quarter or sitting on a fixed rate that ends in 2026.

Q1 · 2026

Why business-purchase financing is easier than it was twelve months ago, and harder than it looks.

— Craig Walker

Twelve months ago, business-purchase financing was difficult to write outside the major bank business divisions. That has changed — second-tier and specialist lenders are back in the market. But the structuring requirements have quietly become more important.

The misread I see most often is treating a business purchase like a property purchase. The financing structure is different, the assessment lens is different, and the security position is different. Get those right early — usually before signing the heads of agreement — and the rest is straightforward.

For anyone seriously evaluating an acquisition in the next six months: budget a conversation with us before you commit to the structure. It's the one piece of work that gets harder the later it's started.

Q4 · 2025

The conversation most refinancers are not having with their accountant.

— Ben Walker

Most refinances are organised between the borrower and the broker. The accountant gets a notification at the end. There's a better order, especially for anyone with investment property or who has restructured ownership in the last few years.

Three things to flag with your accountant before the refinance, not after: the interest deductibility implications of any loan splits, whether the timing of the refinance affects current-year tax position, and whether the new structure changes anything for an SMSF property if you have one.

Where we work coordinated, we usually save the client one or two structural decisions they would have spent a year working around. It's not a big change. It just needs to happen in the right order.
